The Research

What is a 3rd Space?

Sociologist Ray Oldenburg spent his career studying where community actually happens. His answer: not at home, and not at work.

Home is your first place. Work is your second. A 3rd Space is everything else — the recurring place you return to on purpose, not because you have to, but because it's yours. A bowling league. A dance floor. A table full of people who show up for the same game every week.

Oldenburg's research found that real third places share the same handful of traits: they're neutral ground, they level the playing field regardless of who you are outside that place, and conversation — not the activity itself — is the real point.

Most adults had this automatically in school. Almost none of us do by default once it ends. That gap is the actual reason making friends gets so hard as an adult — not a personal failing, a structural one.

Oldenburg, R. (1989). The Great Good Place — the research behind this framework.

Why I Do This

Lived experience, not just theory.

I spent 24 years isolated. Not by circumstance — by default, the way most people quietly are, without ever naming it.

I didn't fix that by reading about it. I fixed it by showing up — to a bowling alley, a dance floor, a card table, a community band — over and over, on purpose, until those places became mine.
